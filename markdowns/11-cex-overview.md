#  Centralized Exchanges (CEX)

Centralized Exchanges (CEXs) are the **most common gateway** for buying, selling, and trading cryptocurrency. They operate similarly to traditional stock exchanges but for digital assets, offering high liquidity, fiat integration, and custodial services.

---

##  What Is a CEX?

A **Centralized Exchange (CEX)** is a platform where:
- Users deposit funds into platform-controlled wallets
- Orders are matched and executed off-chain via an internal engine
- The platform acts as an intermediary, custodian, and liquidity provider

---

##  Core CEX Functions

| Function         | Description                                        |
|------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| Spot Trading     | Buy/sell crypto assets at market prices            |
| Margin Trading   | Borrowed funds to increase trade size              |
| Derivatives      | Futures, options, and perpetual contracts          |
| Staking-as-a-Service | Earn yield while platform retains custody     |
| On/Off-Ramp      | Integrate fiat (USD, EUR) through banks/cards      |

---

##  Benefits of CEX Platforms

- Deep liquidity and fast execution
- Fiat integration (banks, cards)
- Customer support and fraud protection
- Familiar UX for retail users
- Often offer mobile apps + API access

---

##  CEX Risks and Limitations

- **Custodial risk** – you don’t control your private keys
- **Censorship risk** – accounts may be frozen, geo-restricted
- **Regulatory exposure** – subject to local law (can be banned or sanctioned)
- **Transparency gaps** – off-chain matching = unverifiable execution
- **Collapse risk** – as seen in FTX, Mt. Gox, QuadrigaCX

> “Not your keys, not your crypto.”

##  CEX vs DEX Summary

| Attribute         | CEX                          | DEX                          |
|------------------|------------------------------|------------------------------|
| Custody          | Platform-controlled          | Self-custody                 |
| Governance       | Corporate                    | DAO-based                    |
| Order Matching   | Off-chain, internal engine   | On-chain, via AMM            |
| Transparency     | Partial                      | Full                         |
| Fiat Access      | Yes                          | No (unless wrapped/bridged)  |
